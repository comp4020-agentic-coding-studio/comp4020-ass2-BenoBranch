industry: Directory assistance (411 / local operator services)
pitch: >-
  Directory assistance is a solved problem wearing a human costume. An
  operator's entire function -- match a spoken name to a listing, read back a
  number -- is a lookup, and lookups are what computers are for. Automated
  directory assistance systems already handle the majority of call volume in
  every market that deploys them, at a fraction of the marginal cost, with
  zero hold time and zero attitude. This is not disruption; it is correction.
  Retiring the human tier doesn't remove a service, it removes the delay.
migrationPath: >-
  Callers are routed through speech recognition to a structured lookup against
  the same listings database operators used, with a text-to-speech readback
  and an SMS fallback for ambiguous matches; the operator queue becomes a
  low-traffic escalation path for the recognizer's low-confidence cases only.
breakingChanges:
  economic: >-
    Directory assistance employed roughly 100,000 operators across North
    America at its peak; automation and the rise of search engines collapsed
    that number to near zero within two decades, with almost none of the
    difference absorbed by adjacent telecom roles. The jobs didn't move, they
    stopped existing, and the workforce was disproportionately women who had
    entered clerical telecom work as one of the few stable office-adjacent
    careers open to them.
  functional: >-
    An operator disambiguated names a database gets wrong: misheard spellings,
    recently changed numbers, businesses known by a nickname instead of their
    listed name, and callers who didn't know the exact information they were
    asking for and needed a human to ask a clarifying question back.
    Automated systems handle the clean majority of queries well and silently
    fail the messy remainder, which simply stopped being served rather than
    being visibly declined.
rollbackPlan: >-
  None exists at scale. The operator workforce, the training pipeline, and the
  physical exchange infrastructure that supported it were fully
  decommissioned; reconstructing it would mean rebuilding an occupation from
  zero, not restoring a paused one.
---

This week is the exception to how the rest of the course works: instead of
auditing a live pitch, it audits one that already ran to completion. Directory
assistance was automated out of existence between the 1980s and the 2000s, so
every claim below can be checked against what actually happened rather than
argued about in the abstract. That's the point of putting it first --- the
notice format below (pitch, migration path, breaking changes, rollback plan)
is introduced here on a case with a known answer, so you can calibrate what a
honest breaking-changes section looks like before you have to write one about
something still in progress.

## Outline

- why this week's notice describes something that already happened, not a
  live proposal
- reading the notice below in two registers: the pitch and migration path are
  written the way the original sales case actually sounded; breaking changes
  is not
- what "no rollback plan" looks like when it's true, not just pessimistic
- what the audit session is looking for when you write your own notice later
  in the course
