  economic: >-
    This course was staffed, all semester, by a convenor and teaching team
    whose actual labour was choosing which industries to feature, checking

    the parts of the job that don't show up in the four-part template's
    shape. A model that reproduces the template's shape does not reproduce
    that labour, and a reader who can't tell the difference from the outside
    has no way to price what's missing.

    Every notice this semester paired a confident pitch with a plain audit

    mechanism for forming. A generated twelfth notice would read exactly like
    the previous eleven and be accountable to nothing, because the template
    was never the point; the checking behind it was.

  reverses a migration. It's a description of a check that has to keep
  happening.

The last notice turns the format on the course itself. Everything true of
the previous eleven industries --- a confident pitch, a plain audit, a
rollback plan honest about what it can and can't undo --- is true of the
practice of writing these notices too, including the fact that this one was
written by an AI coding agent under a human's direction, following rules
that human wrote down in `CLAUDE.md` before any content existed. That's not
a twist ending; it's the same check applied one level up, and the point of
putting it last is that you now have eleven weeks of practice reading for
exactly this.

## Outline

- what changes, and what doesn't, when the "industry" being deprecated is
  the method the whole course has used
- why this week's rollback plan describes an ongoing check rather than an
  action, and how that differs from every rollback plan before it
- the Sunset Review Board (due this week): defending or blocking a notice
  with the same standard this lecture just applied to itself
