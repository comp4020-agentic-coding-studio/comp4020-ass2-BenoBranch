pitch: >-
  Translation is a mapping problem between two symbol systems that already
  have millions of aligned example pairs to train on. Real-time speech
  translation now runs at low enough latency for live interpreting, handles
  dozens of language pairs a single interpreter never could, and scales to
  every meeting a human interpreter couldn't physically be in at once.
migrationPath: >-
  Conference and clinical audio streams are transcribed and translated in
  real time through a speech-to-speech pipeline; human interpreters are
  retained only for the highest-stakes settings (legal proceedings,
  diplomatic sessions) where liability requires a named professional.
breakingChanges:
  economic: >-
    Interpreting was one of relatively few skilled professions where fluent
    bilingualism from a non-elite educational background was the primary
    credential, giving a viable specialist career to people whose language
    skill came from lived migration rather than formal training. Collapsing
    routine-tier interpreting narrows that path specifically, at the same
    time as the highest-stakes tier this migration isn't touching requires
    increasingly formal certification to access.
  functional: >-
    An interpreter mediates register, not just words --- softening a blunt
    clinical diagnosis for a frightened patient, or preserving a deliberately
    evasive answer's evasiveness for a tribunal that needs to see it. A
    translation pipeline optimized for accuracy against the source text has
    no model of what the listener needs preserved versus softened, and
    that's a judgment call embedded in every interpreted sentence, not an
    edge case.
rollbackPlan: >-
  High-stakes settings already require a human interpreter and will go on
  doing so, so nothing there needs restoring. The harder-to-reverse loss is
  at the routine tier: the interpreters who would have grown into
  high-stakes work no longer get the volume of practice that used to be how
  they got there, so the pipeline feeding the tier this notice says it isn't
  touching is quietly drying up anyway.
---

First notice back from the break, and the first where the rollback plan
isn't simply "harder" than the early weeks --- it's honest about a mechanism
(the practice pipeline feeding the high-stakes tier) that means "we didn't
touch the top tier" doesn't mean the top tier is safe. Read the rollback
plan closely; it's doing more work than it looks like it's doing.

## Outline

- why "we're only automating the routine tier" doesn't mean the expert tier
