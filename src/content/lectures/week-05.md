industry: Local journalism
pitch: >-
  Local reporting is mostly assembling public information --- meeting
  minutes, court filings, permit applications, police blotters --- into
  readable copy on a deadline. A language model with API access to those same
  public feeds drafts the story before the reporter's coffee is done, covers
  every council meeting in the circulation area simultaneously, and never
  needs the beat explained twice.
migrationPath: >-
  Public-record feeds and a local-events calendar are piped into a drafting
  model, which produces first-pass stories tagged by beat; a much smaller
  editorial desk reviews, adds original reporting where warranted, and
  publishes.
breakingChanges:
  economic: >-
    Local newsrooms were already the most cost-pressured tier of journalism
    before this migration, and the beat-reporter role it displaces was often
    the only reporting job in the region at all. Where the model absorbs the
    routine-coverage majority, the remaining edited-and-original-reporting
    tier isn't a smaller version of the same newsroom, it's a different, much
    thinner institution with a different relationship to the community it
    covers.
  functional: >-
    A beat reporter attends the meeting the public record doesn't fully
    capture --- who argued for what, what wasn't said, which resident's
    question changed the room. The model can only draft from what was
    recorded, so the coverage that survives is exactly the routine kind, and
    the accountability journalism that depends on someone in the room asking
    a follow-up question is what quietly stops getting produced, not what
    gets automated.
rollbackPlan: >-
  A community can fund a human beat reporter again, and many are trying to,
  through nonprofit and reader-funded models. But the institutional knowledge
  of a beat --- who to call, what's normal, what's not --- accrues over years
  on the job, and a newsroom that let that knowledge lapse for a decade is
  rebuilding a relationship with its community, not just a headcount.
---

This week's breaking-changes section makes a claim that's easy to skim past:
the model doesn't just do a worse version of the reporter's job, it does a
narrower job that gets called by the same name. Notice that the functional
breaking change isn't "the writing is worse" --- it's that a whole category
of story stops being produced at all, silently, because nothing in the
pitch's description of the task ever included it.

## Outline

- the gap between "assembling public information into copy" and what a beat
  reporter's job actually is, and why the pitch's own framing hides it
- how "a thinner institution with a different relationship to its community"
  differs from "the same institution, smaller"
- why this week's rollback plan is about relationships and institutional
  knowledge rather than headcount or budget
