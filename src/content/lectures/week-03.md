industry: Long-haul trucking
pitch: >-
  Long-haul freight is a long, monotonous control-loop task: hold a lane, hold
  a speed, respond to what's ahead. Autonomous highway systems already log
  more consecutive incident-free miles than any single driver could pull in a
  career, without a mandatory rest break in the arrangement.
migrationPath: >-
  Fleets replace long-haul legs with autonomous highway convoys and keep human
  drivers only for the first and last mile through depots and delivery docks.
breakingChanges:
  economic: >-
    Long-haul driving was one of the last blue-collar jobs paying a
    middle-class wage without a further credential, concentrated in regions
    with few comparable alternatives. Removing the long-haul leg removes that
    wage tier specifically, not just a task, and leaves the lower-paid
    first-and-last-mile work as the only tier still open to drivers.
  functional: >-
    A human driver notices a shifted load, a strange engine sound, or a local
    hazard a map doesn't have yet, and adapts to a situation no policy
    anticipated. Removing the driver removes that noticing, not just the
    steering, and the convoy's response to an unmapped hazard is exactly as
    good as its last map update.
rollbackPlan: >-
  Human long-haul driving can restart on any lane at will --- the trucks and
  the roads don't prevent it. But the drivers who left the industry for other
  work don't return at the same wage, and the training pipelines that
  produced them have mostly closed in the meantime.
---

Same four-part shape as week 1, applied to a mid-migration industry instead
of a resolved one. Notice how much shorter and more confident the rollback
plan reads here than it will by the back half of the semester --- this is
still early-arc territory, where reversing course is inconvenient, not
structurally impossible.
