industry: Fruit & vegetable harvest labour
pitch: >-
  Harvest labour is seasonal, physically punishing, and chronically
  understaffed even with a temporary-visa workforce built specifically to
  fill it. Computer-vision-guided harvesting robots now pick soft fruit at
  commercial speed without bruising, work through the night in temperatures
  human crews can't sustain, and never call in sick during the two-week
  window a crop has to be picked in.
migrationPath: >-
  Fields are fitted with row-following harvesting units guided by
  ripeness-detection cameras; human crews are retained for varieties and
  terrain the units can't yet handle, and for quality-control spot-checks
  behind the machines.
breakingChanges:
  economic: >-
    Harvest labour supports a seasonal migration circuit --- workers, often
    visa-tied, who plan a year's income around a sequence of harvests across
    regions and crops. Automating even the varieties a robot handles well
    removes those specific weeks of income from that circuit without
    offering the same workers a substitute, since the remaining human-harvest
    work is concentrated in the crops and terrain that are hardest, least
    mechanized, and worst-paid precisely because they were never the easy
    cases to automate first.
  functional: >-
    A human picker adapts pressure and grip per individual fruit based on a
    texture and colour judgement built from a season of picking that exact
    variety, and can work an oddly-shaped or wind-damaged row a routed
    machine wasn't tuned for. Robotic harvesters are validated crop-by-crop
    and terrain-by-terrain, so "harvesting is automated" is true for a
    narrowing list of specific, favourable cases and false everywhere else,
    even though the pitch generalizes across the whole category.
rollbackPlan: >-
  Human crews can be hired back for any field at any time --- the equipment
  doesn't prevent it. What doesn't reverse quickly is the visa-tied seasonal
  workforce's own planning: workers who lost a harvest's income from their
  annual circuit two seasons ago have often already committed to a different
  circuit, a different country, or a different kind of work, and don't
  reappear the season a grower decides to rehire.
---

This week's functional breaking change makes a specific, checkable claim
about scope: the pitch describes "harvesting," but the migration only
reaches soft fruit in favourable rows, and generalizes from there. That gap
--- between what a pitch names and what a migration path actually covers ---
is worth watching for in every notice this course has issued, not just this
one.
