industry: Call-centre customer support
pitch: >-
  Most contact-centre volume is the same forty questions, asked in nine
  hundred different ways. A conversational agent trained on every past ticket
  resolves the routine share of that volume immediately, at any hour, in any
  of the languages the training data covers, without putting a caller through
  hold music first.
migrationPath: >-
  Inbound calls and chats are triaged by an intent classifier; resolvable
  intents get answered end-to-end by the agent, and only genuinely novel or
  emotionally escalated contacts are routed to a much smaller human tier.
breakingChanges:
  economic: >-
    Contact-centre work was one of the largest entry-level employment
    categories in regions where call centres cluster, often the first formal
    job for young or newly-arrived workers. Cutting the routine-resolution
    majority of volume removes the bulk of that entry tier's hours, not a
    proportional slice across all workers, and does so fastest in the
    regional economies most dependent on a single centre as a local employer.
  functional: >-
    A human agent can hear that a caller is not actually asking the question
    they're asking --- that the billing complaint is really about a life
    event, that the return request is really about being talked out of
    returning --- and route the conversation accordingly. A classifier
    resolves the stated intent and nothing else, so the calls that most
    needed a person are exactly the ones the automated system correctly
    avoids escalating too late to help.
rollbackPlan: >-
  Centres can re-staff the tier they cut, and the skills involved don't
  require years of training to rebuild. The harder cost is reputational,
  not operational --- customers who learn a brand's default channel is a bot
  the first time they need real help are slow to trust the human tier when
  it's reinstated.
---

This is the first notice you're reading without knowing in advance how the
story ends --- call-centre automation is live and expanding right now, not a
completed historical case like week 1's. Read it the same way regardless:
check whether the breaking-changes section earns its claims, and whether the
rollback plan is honest about what re-staffing would actually cost versus
what it would take to rebuild trust.
