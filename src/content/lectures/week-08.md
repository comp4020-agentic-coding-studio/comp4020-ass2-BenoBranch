industry: K-12 formative grading & tutoring
pitch: >-
  Formative feedback --- the ungraded draft comments, the practice-set
  corrections, the "try this differently" notes --- is high-volume and
  low-stakes exactly where a model is strongest. An always-available tutor
  gives every student the individualized, patient, never-frustrated
  attention that a teacher managing thirty students physically cannot give
  each of them every day.
migrationPath: >-
  Students submit drafts and practice work to a tutoring model that gives
  immediate formative feedback and adaptive practice; the teacher's time is
  reallocated to summative assessment, whole-class instruction, and
  intervention for students the model flags as stuck.
breakingChanges:
  economic: >-
    Formative feedback and one-on-one tutoring time were among the only
    parts of teaching load that scaled down class-size pressure by actually
    costing a teacher more individual minutes per student. Removing that
    load doesn't reduce a teacher's total hours proportionally, because the
    summative and whole-class work it's reallocated to doesn't compress the
    same way, and early results in adopting districts show teacher hours per
    student holding flat or rising rather than falling as the pitch implies.
  functional: >-
    A teacher notices when a wrong answer reveals a specific misconception
    versus a careless slip, and knows which of this particular student's
    other struggles it connects to. A tutoring model responds to the answer
    in front of it, patiently and correctly, without the years of watching
    this specific child that make a human teacher's "try this differently"
    land as recognition rather than correction --- and formative feedback
    that doesn't feel recognized is feedback students routinely stop
    engaging with, whatever its content.
rollbackPlan: >-
  Teachers can resume giving formative feedback personally at any time
  staffing allows it, so nothing here is structurally irreversible. But a
  cohort of students who spent their early formative years being corrected
  by a patient tutor and rarely by a person who knows them may arrive at the
  point of reversal already having learned that feedback is something to
  complete, not something to be known through --- and that's a habit of
  mind, not a staffing level.
---

Notice the economic breaking-changes section this week doesn't argue against
the pitch's numbers, it argues that the pitch measured the wrong thing:
teacher-hours-per-student wasn't supposed to change, and the early evidence
says it hasn't fallen. That's a different, harder-to-dismiss kind of
objection than "the savings are overstated," and it's the first time this
course has used it.

## Outline

- the difference between disputing a pitch's numbers and disputing what the
  numbers were measuring in the first place
- why "recognition rather than correction" is doing real conceptual work in
  the functional breaking-changes section, not just softer language
- what a rollback plan that's technically reversible but concedes a lasting
  effect on a "habit of mind" is telling you about how to read the ones still
  ahead
