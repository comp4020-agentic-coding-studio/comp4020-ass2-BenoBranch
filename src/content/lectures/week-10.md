pitch: >-
  Loneliness in aged care is a staffing-ratio problem --- not enough hours in
  a shift for meaningful conversation on top of physical care. A companion
  agent is available at 3am as readily as 3pm, remembers every previous
  conversation with perfect fidelity, never has a bad day that shortens its
  patience, and costs a fraction of an additional care worker's wage.
migrationPath: >-
  Residents are given a conversational companion device for social
  interaction between scheduled care visits; care-worker rostering is set to
  the physical-care minimum, with the companion agent covering the
  conversational and check-in load a fuller roster used to cover informally.
breakingChanges:
  economic: >-
    The conversational load this notice reassigns was never a costed line
    item --- it was the informal part of a care worker's job that made the
    costed physical-care minutes bearable to deliver and to receive, and
    families frequently chose a home partly on staff who "know" a resident.
    Rostering to the bare physical-care minimum on the assumption the agent
    covers the rest doesn't just cut a cost, it removes the slack that made
    the remaining paid minutes humane to work and to receive, for both the
    worker delivering them and the resident on the other end.
  functional: >-
    Companionship that helps with loneliness is, specifically, being known
    by someone who remembers you're a person and not a task, and choosing to
    be there anyway. A companion agent can simulate perfect conversational
    recall without anyone having chosen anything, and residents living with
    dementia in particular have already been documented becoming confused or
    distressed about the relationship's nature over extended use. The thing
    being purchased is not a substitute for the thing it's marketed to
    replace; it is a different experience wearing the same name.
rollbackPlan: >-
  Rosters can be restored to a fuller staffing ratio whenever funding allows
  it, in principle. In practice, the workforce that would staff it has to be
  recruited back into a sector already struggling with turnover and pay,
  against competition for care workers from every other part of the sector
  that didn't make this cut --- and the residents who spent years primarily
  companioned by an agent instead of a person aren't made whole by a staffing
  change that arrives after that time has passed.
---

This is the week the plan flagged as the form under the most strain, and it
should read that way: the breaking-changes section isn't correcting the
pitch's facts, it's disputing whether "companionship" is even the right word
for what's being sold. Notice that the pitch is not written more dishonestly
than earlier weeks --- it's the same confident register applied to a claim
that doesn't hold the same way under it.

## Outline

- why this week's functional breaking change is an argument about naming
  ("a different experience wearing the same name"), not just about quality
- what "informal load that was never costed" means for how you'd write an
  economic breaking-changes section about care work generally
- how to tell, reading only the pitch, that this industry was going to be
  harder to write an honest notice about than weeks 2 through 6 were
