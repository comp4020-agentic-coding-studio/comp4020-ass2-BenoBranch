An audit is not a debate about whether the pitch is right. It's a close
read of one document, checking whether its own four parts agree with each
other. This week's document already ran its course, so there's no argument
to have about the ending --- only the discipline of reading it that way, on
a document where it's cheap to be wrong.

## Before the session

Read this week's notice twice: once straight through as the sales case it's
imitating, once again stopping at every heading. Bring the sentence where the
register audibly changes.

## In the session

Short round of "where's the seam" --- everyone names the sentence they found,
and we check whether the group agrees on where pitch ends and audit begins.
Then we work through the `spec` above as a group, out loud, against the
lecture's own text rather than against what anyone assumes the right answer
is.

## Afterwards

Nothing here is marked. It's the only audit in the course practising on a
case with a known outcome, so what you take from it is the habit --- reading
for the seam --- that the marked audits in later weeks assume you already
have.
