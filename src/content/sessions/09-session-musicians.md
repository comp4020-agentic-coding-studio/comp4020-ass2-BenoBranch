Week 1's audit was a clean split: pitch on one side of the seam, plain audit
on the other, and the group agreed on the sentence. This week's notice
resists that. The breaking-changes section doesn't just correct the pitch,
it undermines the premise the pitch is built on --- and the rollback plan is
neither a real plan nor a flat refusal. The audit skill this session
practises is reading a notice that's honestly ambivalent, without either
flattening it into "obviously bad" or accepting the pitch at face value
because it's well-written.

## Before the session

Read this week's notice against week 1's. Bring one specific difference in
how confidently each rollback plan is written, and don't just say "week 9 is
less confident" --- say what in the text makes it less confident.

## In the session

We read the pitch aloud first, cold, the way a client would hear it. Then we
read the breaking-changes section aloud and check who in the room changes
their mind about the pitch, and on what sentence. Twenty minutes on the
`spec` questions above, in pairs, then a shared list on the board of what
"strained" actually looks like at the sentence level --- this is the
vocabulary your own Notice, due after this session, will be marked against.

## Afterwards

This audit's `spec` lines are the marking language for File Your Own Notice.
If a line here didn't make sense against this week's notice, say so before
you write yours --- it's cheaper to fix now than after the assessment is
submitted.
