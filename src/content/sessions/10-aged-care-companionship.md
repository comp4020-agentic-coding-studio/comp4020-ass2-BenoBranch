This week's notice doesn't dispute the pitch's facts --- it disputes whether
"companionship" is the right word for what's on offer. That's the hardest
kind of audit: no number to check, no timeline to verify, just a claim about
what a word is doing in the pitch versus what it's doing in the audit.

## Before the session

Bring one word from this week's pitch you think is carrying more weight than
the facts underneath it can support.

## In the session

We build, as a group, a short list of what evidence --- interviews,
longitudinal studies, resident and family accounts --- would actually settle
this week's dispute, since the notice itself can't.

## Afterwards

This is the session to revisit if your own Sunset Review Board defence turns
on a naming dispute rather than a factual one --- the standard of evidence is
different, and worth being explicit about.
