The board is the course's own version of the check this week's lecture
describes: someone accountable, reading a notice closely, before it goes
out. You'll do that job for a classmate's notice, and have it done for
yours.

## Before the session

Read your assigned notice the way week 1 taught you to: find the seam, check
whether the parts agree, and bring the specific sentence your case will turn
on.

## In the session

Formal review-board format --- defend or block, then cross-examination from
the room, using only evidence from the notice and whatever sources you
brought. General scepticism ("AI pitches are always overblown") and general
enthusiasm ("automation is just progress") both lose against a specific
sentence.

## Afterwards

This is the last session of the course. The habit it's assessing --- reading
a confident pitch for the seam where the plain audit starts --- is the one
thing every week was actually teaching, regardless of which industry that
week's notice was about.
