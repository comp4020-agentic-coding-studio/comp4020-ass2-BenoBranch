> You're assigned a deprecation notice --- one written by a classmate, or one
> of the semester's twelve. Defend it or block it in front of a review board,
> using nothing but the notice and whatever evidence you bring.

This is the course's own version of the check every lecture has described
all semester: someone accountable, reading a notice closely, before it goes
out. The board makes that check a live event instead of a private habit.

## What you submit

A short written brief (400–600 words) prepared before the session, stating
your position and your three strongest pieces of evidence. In the session
itself, you present and defend that position against cross-examination from
the room. The written brief and the live defence are marked together, as one
piece of work --- a strong brief with a defence that can't survive
questioning is not a strong result, and neither is the reverse.

Held in the last week of semester, after every lecture's notice and every
classmate's filed notice are available to draw evidence from.
