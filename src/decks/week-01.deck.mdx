# Deprecation Notice 01

## Directory Assistance

---

## Before the recognizer existed

Every listed number in a phone company's territory, held in a switchboard
operator's head or a card index at her elbow. You dialled 411, said a name
half-remembered, and a person negotiated the gap between what you said and
what was listed.

That negotiation is the whole job. It's also exactly the part a lookup table
can't do.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> "The Bell System operator ... could locate almost any listing from the
> vaguest of clues." — from a 1970s internal Bell training manual

---

## The pitch, as it was actually made

- speech recognition + a structured database beats a human on cost, latency,
  and consistency, full stop
- the marginal call costs a fraction of a cent instead of an operator's wage
- zero hold time, zero shift roster, zero attrition

Read that again. Every sentence is true. That's what makes this week's case
useful — the pitch doesn't need exaggerating to sound compelling.

---

## What the pitch left out

- ~100,000 operator jobs in North America at the peak, collapsed to near zero
- almost none of that headcount moved sideways into telecom — it stopped
  existing
- the workforce was disproportionately women, for whom this was one of the
  few stable office-adjacent careers open at the time

---

{/* _class: banner */}

## The messy 20%

Misheard names. Recently changed numbers. A business known by a nickname
instead of its listed name. A caller who didn't know the exact thing they
were asking for and needed a human to ask a clarifying question back.

The recognizer doesn't fail loudly on these. It just stops serving them.

---

## Rollback plan: none

Not "difficult." Not "expensive." The training pipeline, the workforce, and
the exchange infrastructure that supported it were fully decommissioned.
There is no paused system to restart — the occupation would have to be
rebuilt from zero.

This is what an honest rollback plan looks like when the honest answer is no.
